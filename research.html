Research

Research Interests

  • Adolescent & child development
  • Radicalization
  • Hate speech
  • Social norms
  • Peer influence
  • Prevention & intervention science
  • Longitudinal methods

A throughline across my work is a proposed integration between developmental science and radicalization studies: bringing the theories, methods, and developmental rigor typically applied to adolescent risk behavior to the study of how young people come to hold extremist or intolerant beliefs. Rather than treating radicalization as a fixed endpoint, I approach it as a developmental process, shaped over time by peer norms, family context, and the online and offline environments adolescents grow up in. My dissertation and current project each reflect a piece of this broader integration.

Dissertation · Expected 2026

Perceived Peer Norms and Adolescent Religious Attitudes: Insights from the Kingdom of Bahrain

Three empirical chapters examining perceived peer norms of religious intolerance, religious open-mindedness, and links to physical and relational bullying in adolescence, including gender differences.

Current Project

Algorithmic Recommendation of Hate Speech Content to Adolescents

With Carol Coimbra Vieira, Ph.D. An age-stratified bot audit of TikTok content recommendations, using a sock-puppet methodology and an LLM-based hate speech classifier to examine differences in exposure to covert and overt hate speech. Currently piloting data collection.

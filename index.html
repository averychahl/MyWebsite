About

I study how peer social norms shape adolescent attitudes and behavior, with a particular focus on religious intolerance, hate speech, and pathways toward radicalization. My work combines longitudinal and social network methods with algorithmic audits to understand how peer contexts, both offline and online, influence youth development.

I am a Ph.D. candidate in Human Development and Family Studies at The Pennsylvania State University, advised by Jennifer L. Maggs and Kelly L. Rulison, and a Visiting Scholar in the Kinship Inequalities Research Group at the Max Planck Institute for Demographic Research, working with Diego Alburez-Gutiérrez.
